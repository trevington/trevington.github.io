FRC 2025 Design

Published: September 16th, 2026

Overview

Reefscape was team 4192 Jaguar Robotics' 14th season. As team captain and design lead, I led our 35 person team to the world championship in Houston, ending a 4 year drought.

Our 2025 Robot Reveal.

Design Criteria

After designing several robot concepts, we landed on the final criteria for our robot.

Our Robot will:

  • Remove algae from the reef
  • Score Coral on three levels
  • Deep climb during endgame
  • Work every match

We also wanted to score algae in the processor to assist in reaching the Coral RP.

Development

The robot development started with a few concepts.

After some exploratory design work, we realized that in order to score on L4 we'd need an elevator or large arm. Because we had no experience mitigating backlash on large arms, we decided to use an elevator to lift the coral.

We had used elevators on our 2023 and 2024 robots, and felt comfortable reusing the elevator architecture.

The arm was more challenging than the elevator. In 2024 our robot's arms had chain tension and backlash issues that limited the performance.

Since our robot needed a human to feed it coral, we quickly built a prototype feeding station.

We used the station to prototype our ramp geometry. An angle of 15° was found to work best (a range of 10° to 25° was tested).

To avoid some of the electrical issues of 2024, we prototyped our elevator's energy chain.

Testing

We made good progress, and our robot was ready 1 week before our first event at Plano.

Plano District Event

We finished qualifications ranked 24th and were selected as the first pick of the 7th alliance. Our alliance was eliminated in the third round of playoffs after going 1-2.

While our robot didn't work as expected (more on that in the next section), we did win the Impact Award, the highest possible award!

Post-Plano

Ramp

Unfortunately during Plano our ramp broke while transporting the robot. While our quick zip-tie fix worked in the moment, we decided to use the opportunity to redesign and rebuild our ramp.

When our ramp broke coral easily became stuck in the chassis. Before Fort Worth we added some chicken-wire to help coral roll off of the robot.

Arm

While our arm worked great with negligible backlash, the bolts transfering torque from the sprocket to the main plates began shearing the SRPP.

We changed the arm's sideplates to use 5052 aluminum instead of SRPP. Since the arm didn't experience any collisions the SRPP's flexibility was not needed.

To coaxialize the arm the design was switched from a bolt pattern to an aluminum spline.

We also changed the elevator's motors from Vortexes to Krakens to get closer to the target ~0.5 second extension time

We also made sure to maximize our practice time before Fort Worth.

Fort Worth District Event

Fort Worth started hot, with our robot improvements and practice making cycle times quick.

Unfortunately our fortune ended with our ramp breaking midway through the competition.

We were able to get our ramp fixed, but we weren't able to reattach the active wheels. This made our intakes less consistent.

8874 selected us in the second round of alliance selection, and we went 1-2 in playoffs after putting up some high scores.

While our robot's performance was underwhelming, we were awarded the Engineering Inspiration Award, the highest award we were elligible for.

Post Fort Worth

Disappointed with our robot's performance, we sought to fix our ramp and get our robot perfected for the Texas State Championship (DCMP).

Ramp

Imediately after Fort Worth a redesign of the ramp started.

Ground Intake

Seeing the importance of scoring on L1, I designed and machined a ground intake within a 1 week span to add to our robot for the state championship.

Autonomous Mode

We weren't satisfied with our auto and spent several days preparing a 2 coral autonomous routine.
